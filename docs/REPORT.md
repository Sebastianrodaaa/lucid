# lucid: report

A looped world model trained toward the nearest legal future, with a learned energy that decides when to stop looping, on a small stochastic arcade game, on CPU. This report lists every run. Numbers come from `results/*.json` only; the tables between the markers are regenerated by `python -m lucid.eval --tables` and checked by `test_lucid.py`.

## Method

**Game** (`lucid/game.py`). 32x32 cells, 8 colours: walls, a 5-cell paddle, a ball, 28 bricks in 4 rows, falling bonuses. Actions left, stay, right. Two events are random. A broken brick drops a bonus in one of its 4 columns, in one of 2 colours (8 outcomes). A lost ball respawns at row 20 in one of 16 columns (16 outcomes). Every other step is deterministic. `legal_futures(state, action)` enumerates the exact set of next states, each equally likely. The simulator is the only oracle: the truth lamp, every legality metric, and the spawn histograms ask it, never the model.

**Data.** Real transitions from the simulator: half the episodes use a scripted player that tracks the ball (random action with probability 0.1), half a uniformly random player; 200-step episodes from random brick layouts. Each sample is (previous frame, current frame, action, next frame) plus, for random steps, all legal next frames. Training seeds use simulator seed 1000 + seed. The test set is a separate 40,000-transition draw with simulator seed 7777.

**Model** (`lucid/model.py`). One refiner for every learned arm, 1,802,305 parameters: two 3x3 convolutions over (previous, current, draft) one-hot frames, 4x4 patches into 64 tokens of width 256, a 2-layer pre-norm transformer encoder shared by every loop, a linear head that adds a logit residual to the draft, and a small energy head (on detached features, so it does not change the trunk) that predicts whether the frame is illegal. Loop k feeds the logits of loop k-1 back in as the draft. Pass 1 is seeded with a Gaussian noise draft, so different seeds can settle on different legal futures.

**Arms** (`lucid/train.py`), all at the same parameter count and the same data:

- **A one-pass**: zero draft, one pass, per-cell cross-entropy to the stored next frame.
- **B nearest-of-16**: one pass from a noise draft; 16 drafts are tried without gradient and the one whose output is closest (lowest NLL) to the stored next frame is trained on (one-step IMLE, our re-implementation).
- **C looped, standard target**: depth sampled from 1 to 8 per batch, earlier loops without gradient, cross-entropy of the last loop to the stored next frame. Evaluated at fixed depth.
- **D looped, proximal target, fixed depth**: the target is the legal future the current prediction already gives the highest likelihood. D is the trunk of E evaluated at a fixed depth 1, 2, 4 or 8. Because E's energy head sees detached features, training it does not change the trunk: a separately trained D would have identical trunk gradients. ponytail: one training run serves both arms.
- **E lucid**: D plus energy halting. Per frame, stop at the first loop where sigmoid(energy) < 0.5, at most 8. The threshold was fixed before evaluation and not tuned on the test set; a sweep is reported separately.
- **F simulator oracle**: the real game. Legal by definition; timed for reference.

**Metrics** (`lucid/eval.py`). A frame is legal when it exactly equals one of the simulator's legal futures (all 1,024 cells). One-step legal rate on the test set, also split into random steps and quiet steps. Closed-loop legal rate: 50 rollouts of 100 steps from real starts with the scripted player; the model is fed its own frames, and the simulator follows the nearest legal future of each dreamed frame to keep judging it. Spawn distribution: 20 real pre-bonus states x 100 noise seeds = 2,000 rollouts per model; total variation of the bonus column distribution from uniform, with illegal frames counted as mass outside the support. Halting: Spearman correlation between loops used and the number of legal futures; illegal rate at the energy's mean loops versus fixed depth at the same mean loops (linear interpolation between depths). CPU latency: batch 1, 4 threads, median of 280 frames after 20 warm-up frames. 95% intervals are percentile bootstrap over frames (1,000 resamples).
